# Newton's second law force accounting for a simulated multi-finger grasp

## Q1: What's the standard approach in robotics/physics-informed ML for decomposing net force on a grasped object into known/commanded components vs. estimated contact reaction forces?

### Takeaway
The standard robotics framework (grasp-matrix / Newton-Euler formalism) splits the net wrench on a grasped object into (a) external wrenches like gravity and a commanded/applied load, and (b) the sum of fingertip contact wrenches mapped through a "grasp matrix" G; contact forces themselves are further split into a null-space "internal/grasping" component that has zero net effect on the object's motion and a complementary "manipulating" component that is what actually produces the object's acceleration. This decomposition is mathematically well-posed and long-established (Yoshikawa, Bicchi, Prattichizzo et al.) — it is not itself the hard part; the hard part in most real-world robotics papers is that the individual contact forces are *unmeasured* and must be estimated, which is a separate estimation problem layered on top of the decomposition.

### Cited Findings
- A 1997 IEEE CDC paper (Prattichizzo, Mercorelli, Bicchi, Vicino) defines internal grasp forces as "self-balanced forces" that have "no action on the object dynamics," placing them formally in the null space of the grasp matrix; it also shows power/kinematically-deficient grasps produce a non-zero null space of the Jacobian transpose (ker(Jᵀ) ≠ 0) — [Source](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/1997_CDC.pdf)
- A later summary of Bicchi's work states that for a kinematically deficient hand, only a subset of internal grasping forces is "actively controllable"; the internal-force subspace is decomposed into active and passive internal-force subspaces — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.06558)
- A paper on synergistically underactuated hands describes the "Fundamental Grasp Matrix" (FGM) and its canonical form, which reveals the subspaces of controllable internal forces vs. controllable object displacements — [Source](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/node/2888)
- Yoshikawa's classical framing (cited secondhand in a triple-manipulator internal-loading paper) separates "grasping forces" (internal, must satisfy friction constraints, do not affect end-effector motion) from "manipulating forces" (the component that does move the object) — [Source](https://koreascience.kr/article/CFKO200333239337065.pdf)
- A hand-object manipulation paper (arXiv 2006.02996, "Manipulation with Shared Grasping") formulates the hand-object system's equilibrium using stacked contact-wrench and velocity vectors mapped through friction-cone edges against the robot's control force, with the free object's own force balance set to zero in the hand frame so that control force carries the load — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.02996)
- A 1995 International Journal of Robotics Research paper resolves each grasp-point contact force into normal and tangential components under a frictional point-contact model, relating contact forces to contact geometry — [Source](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/027836499501400104)
- MuJoCo's own documented rigid-body equation of motion is M·v̇ + c = τ + Jᵀ·f, where "c … includes Coriolis, centrifugal and gravitational forces," and separately, "applied forces" (distinct from contact forces) are stored in `mjData.qfrc_applied` and `mjData.xfrc_applied` — [Source](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)
- A 2025 PINN paper for robot-arm dynamics (Sun et al., *Frontiers of Information Technology & Electronic Engineering*) adds an external-force term to a physics-informed network because "existing PINN frameworks handle external forces poorly," embedding a current-to-torque mapping as a physics prior for industrial arms (UR5/UR10e) that lack joint torque sensors — [Source](https://www.fitee.zjujournals.com/rc-pub/front/front-article/download/145760669/lowqualitypdf/Physics-informed%20neural%20networks%20for%20the%20prediction%20of%20robot%20dynamics%20considering%20motor%20and%20external%20force%20couplings.pdf)

### Inferences
- For this project's object (the grasped object being pulled), the decomposition is conceptually simple compared to the dexterous-manipulation literature above: there is no "manipulation" happening (the hand is not trying to move the object through internal/grasping-force control), so the full Bicchi/Yoshikawa internal-vs-manipulating split is more machinery than is needed. The project's actual equation is just object-level Newton's second law: m·a_object = m·g + F_pull(t) + Σᵢ F_contact,i(t), with no null-space subtlety required unless the model wants to also reason about grip-force redundancy across the 5 fingertips.
- The literature's "hard part" (estimating unmeasured contact forces from indirect signals) is the part that does *not* apply inside MuJoCo, since contact forces are directly queryable there (see Q2) — this reframes the originally-flagged "ambiguity" as potentially a non-issue specific to the simulated setting, not a generally unsolved problem the student would need to invent a solution for.

### Gaps
- Did not find a single canonical textbook-style free-body-diagram derivation with full notation; Murray/Li/Sastry's *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation* and Siciliano et al.'s *Robotics: Modelling, Planning and Control* are the standard textbook references for the grasp-matrix chapter but were not independently fetched/verified in this session.
- The arXiv 2006.02996 PDF could not be parsed directly (binary/compressed stream); the equation description above is drawn from the WebSearch tool's own summary of it, not a verified direct quote — treat with slightly lower confidence than the other citations.

## Q2: Given MuJoCo simulation (where ground-truth contact forces ARE actually queryable), what's the right way to use this for a training-time physics residual while being honest about what would transfer to a real robot that lacks ground-truth contact force access?

### Takeaway
MuJoCo directly exposes per-contact 6D force/torque via the documented `mj_contactForce` function (verified from the primary docs), already resolved into normal + tangential(+torsional/rolling) components in the contact frame — so computing a Newton's-second-law residual at training time is a straightforward *engineering* task, not an open research problem. The honest, literature-consistent pattern for using such sim-only ground truth is to make it a **training-time-only auxiliary loss term that never becomes a model input or a deployment-time dependency** — exactly the pattern used by real sim-to-real tactile-sensing papers (which must substitute a calibrated proxy or a differentiable self-calibration trick for ground-truth force once on real hardware) and by physics-informed RL work like PIPER (which reads exact dynamics terms from the simulator only during training, adding zero cost/dependency at deployment).

### Cited Findings
- `mj_contactForce` is documented with signature `void mj_contactForce(const mjModel* m, const mjData* d, int id, mjtNum result[6]);` and described as extracting "6D force:torque given contact id, in the contact frame" — [Source](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/APIreference/APIfunctions.html)
- MuJoCo's contact frame convention: "The first (x) axis of this frame is the contact normal direction," with the remaining axes spanning the tangent plane; for `condim` 3 or higher the constraint force has normal and tangential-friction parts, and for `condim` 4 or 6 it additionally has torsional and rolling parts — [Source](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)
- MuJoCo's equation of motion M·v̇ + c = τ + Jᵀ·f is solved each step by the simulator itself (c bundles gravity/Coriolis/centrifugal; applied forces are tracked separately in `qfrc_applied`/`xfrc_applied`) — [Source](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)
- Tacmap (arXiv 2602.21625): in simulation, contact force ground truth comes "from the physics engine's contact sensors," but on real hardware the paper trains a ResNet regressor against readings from "an external high-precision force sensor" as the real-world ground-truth substitute — i.e., the real robot does not get free access to the same ground truth and needs its own calibration apparatus — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21625)
- A Center-of-Pressure representation paper (arXiv/EmergentMind 2605.28812) avoids needing real ground-truth force at all: its sensor calibration "uses differentiable dynamics to estimate taxel orientations," letting a multi-fingered-hand policy transfer zero-shot without ever measuring true contact force on hardware — [Source](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.28812)
- Sim-to-real optical tactile sensing (arXiv 2012.11295) generates training labels from finite-element simulation with no real images, and the authors state a "gap between simulated and real force predictions remains," left for future work — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.11295)
- "Learning the sense of touch in simulation" (arXiv 2003.02640) reports that after one real-world calibration point, error becomes comparable to commercial force-sensor resolution, attributing remaining gap mainly to image-processing artifacts and modeling approximations — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.02640)
- Dexterous force-based grasping (arXiv 2607.04940) names "tactile sensing, complex in-hand contact, and actuator dynamics" as the main sim-to-real gap sources, and mitigates by randomizing friction, damping, and restitution during training rather than assuming ground-truth force transfers — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04940)
- PIPER (arXiv 2603.14469, "Physics-Informed Policy Optimization via Analytic Dynamics Regularization") adds a differentiable Lagrangian-dynamics residual, built from the exact M, C, G terms "extracted from the robot's simulator description at runtime," as an extra loss term during policy optimization only — "existing simulators and core RL algorithms need no changes," implying the physics term is not needed/used at deployment time — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14469)

### Inferences
- The right design is: compute r(t) = m·a_object(t) − [m·g + F_pull(t) + Σᵢ F_contact,i(t)] using MuJoCo's ground-truth, per-contact `mj_contactForce` output plus the object's simulator-exposed acceleration, strictly as a *loss term during training/fine-tuning*. The TCN's input vector should never include the raw per-contact ground-truth force (this is consistent with the project's existing leakage-safe-features rule excluding obj_x/y/z, displacement, and slip_speed — ground-truth contact force is the same category of "answer key" signal and should be treated identically: usable for a loss, never as a feature).
- Because the project's current closed-loop deployment target is still the same MuJoCo simulation (not yet real OrcaHand hardware), ground-truth contact-force access is presently "free" and this isn't yet a sim-to-real problem — but the ISEF writeup should explicitly flag, citing the Tacmap/CoP pattern above, that porting this physics-residual term to a real hand would require either a calibrated tactile sensor as a force proxy or a self-calibration scheme, and that this is future work, not solved here.
- PIPER's "training-time-only, zero deployment-cost" pattern is a direct template for the project's already-decided idea 2 (on-policy fine-tuning using a physics-residual loss on visited closed-loop states, no labels needed): the fine-tuning phase can freely use `mj_contactForce` ground truth since it only ever updates weights, never becomes a runtime input.

### Gaps
- Could not get a verified primary-source quote for `cfrc_ext` specifically (whether it's a body-level aggregate of contact forces only, or also folds in applied/connect-constraint forces, and its exact frame) — repeated fetch attempts against the MuJoCo docs were truncated or didn't reach that field. `mj_contactForce` (confirmed, per-contact, contact-frame) is a safer API to anchor the design on instead of `cfrc_ext`, since it is directly verified in this research.
- Did not find a paper doing literally this project's setup (slip-prediction TCN + per-fingertip Newton's-law training residual on a simulated grasp-and-pull); the closest analogues found (PIPER for RL regularization, Sun et al. for arm-dynamics PINNs) are structurally similar but not a direct precedent.

## Q3: Is there a simplified, tractable version achievable by a high schooler, or should this be scoped down/deferred further?

### Takeaway
The force-accounting decomposition itself is tractable and should no longer be treated as a blocking ambiguity — in simulation every term of m·a_object = m·g + F_pull + Σ F_contact is directly queryable, so resolving "the ambiguity" is bookkeeping, not new science. However, there is a more fundamental tractability concern the earlier deferral didn't surface: because MuJoCo's forward-dynamics solver computes trajectories by *enforcing* M·v̇ + c = τ + Jᵀ·f at every step, a residual computed against the simulator's own logged ground truth is likely to be ≈0 everywhere, in both slip and stable trials — carrying the same "no discriminative signal" character the project already found for raw summed tactile forces. The literature's actual use of Newton's-law residuals is almost always on a *model's own predicted* quantities (what the network outputs), not as a violation check on data a constraint solver already made self-consistent — so the recommended scope-down is to fold the decomposition work into the already-planned idea 2 (on-policy fine-tuning) rather than pursue it as a new standalone physics feature/loss.

### Cited Findings
- (See Q2) MuJoCo's documented equation of motion M·v̇ + c = τ + Jᵀ·f is the equation the simulator's forward-dynamics step numerically solves at each timestep — [Source](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html)
- Physics-informed deep learning without labeled data (arXiv 2006.08472) trains a network by minimizing residuals of the governing PDE plus boundary/initial conditions — i.e., the residual is evaluated on the *network's* candidate solution, not on ground-truth simulation data that already satisfies the PDE by construction — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08472)
- PIPER (arXiv 2603.14469) applies its dynamics residual to "nudge policy updates toward dynamically consistent solutions" — the residual measures how far the policy's *chosen action* would violate the equations of motion, not how far logged simulator trajectories deviate from Newton's law — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14469)
- A semi-supervised physics-constraint paper (arXiv 2109.06407) enforces physics constraints on both labeled and unlabeled state-space points via an augmented-Lagrangian method, reporting a "constraint loss gap of four orders of magnitude between variants" — again constraints are imposed on model outputs/predicted states, not used as a pass/fail filter on already-consistent simulated data — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.06407)

### Inferences
- Because MuJoCo's integrator solves for contact/constraint forces f such that the equation of motion holds (within small numerical/compliance tolerance) at every step regardless of whether a trial ends in slip, a naive "F=ma violation" signal computed from logged ground truth should be expected to look flat/uninformative across slip vs. stable trials — mechanistically consistent with, and a plausible explanation for, the project's own already-observed diagnostic finding that summed normal force, tangential force, and torque_mag are flat and overlapping between classes. This is my own inference from the documented equation of motion, not a claim found explicitly stated in any fetched source — flagging it as inference rather than fact.
- A tractable, non-redundant use of the Newton's-law decomposition for a high schooler in the remaining timeframe is therefore not "add a new per-timestep residual feature/loss on logged trials" (likely to reproduce the existing null result with a clearer explanation of *why* it's null), but to reuse the same decomposition machinery (m·g trivial, F_pull already a model input, Σ F_contact,i computed via `mj_contactForce`, a_object from simulator state) as the residual inside the already-decided idea 2 fine-tuning loop — there, the residual is evaluated on states the *policy itself* visits/produces in closed loop, where genuine model-vs-physics mismatch (not solver self-consistency) is the thing being penalized, matching how PIPER and the PINN literature actually use these residuals.
- This scoping is achievable within the stated "high schooler, not full-time, mid-October to March" budget specifically because the hard part (estimating unknown contact forces) is skipped — MuJoCo gives them for free — and the remaining work is wiring `mj_contactForce` + object acceleration + the known pull force into the fine-tuning loss already planned for idea 2, rather than researching or inventing a new estimator.

### Gaps
- No literature was found that directly validates (or refutes) the specific claim that constraint-solver-generated trajectories yield a near-zero, non-discriminative F=ma residual when checked against ground truth — this is reasoned from first principles/the documented equations of motion, not confirmed by a citation, and should be treated as a hypothesis the student could cheaply verify empirically (e.g., compute r(t) on a handful of existing logged trials) before committing design time to it.
- No source was found specifically addressing feasibility/scope-sizing of a physics-informed-ML sub-project for a high-school ISEF timeline; the time-budget conclusions above are inferred from the general pattern in the cited papers (all of which describe the residual/constraint machinery as a modest, almost "free" addition to an existing training loop once the base network and simulator are already working, which is the project's current state) rather than from any source that discusses student-researcher timelines.
